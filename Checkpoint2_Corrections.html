1. Lewis Structures, Ion Electron Counts & VSEPR Shape

Valence Electron Rule for Polyatomic Ions: Electrons carry negative charge. Anions (-) ADD electrons; Cations (+) SUBTRACT electrons. Example: NH4+ = 5 + 4(1) - 1 = 8 e-; OH- = 6 + 1 + 1 = 8 e-.

Octet Verification: Main-group atoms (C, N, O, F, S, P, Cl) require 8 surrounding electrons (bonding pairs + lone pairs).

2. Ionic Naming, Polyatomic Ions & Coulomb's Law

Naming Rules: Metal + nonmetal = Ionic (NO Greek prefixes like di- or tri-; MgCl2 = magnesium chloride). Transition metal Roman numerals like (III) state the ion charge (Fe3+ + 3 Br- → FeBr3), NEVER the metal subscript. Always reduce ratios (Cu2+ + SO42- → CuSO4).

Core Polyatomic Ions: SO42- (sulfate), CO32- (carbonate), NO3- (nitrate), OH- (hydroxide), PO43- (phosphate), NH4+ (ammonium).

Coulomb's Law (Melting Point): Lattice attraction grows with higher ion charges and smaller ion radii. Step 1: Compare charges (|+2 × -2| = 4 beats |+1 × -1| = 1, so CaO > LiF). Step 2: Only break ties using smaller radius (Cl- < I- → NaCl > NaI).

3. Intermolecular Forces, Isomers & Material Properties

IMF Hierarchy (strongest to weakest): Hydrogen bonding (molecules with H directly bonded to N, O, or F → e.g., ethanol CH3CH2OH vs. dimethyl ether CH3OCH3) > Dipole-Dipole (polar molecules like CH3Cl) > London Dispersion Forces (only IMF in nonpolar CH4).

Structural Isomers: Same molecular formula (e.g., C4H10 or C2H6O), different atom connectivity → different boiling points & properties.

Conductivity & Brittleness: Ionic solids lock ions in a fixed lattice (nonconducting; hammering shifts layers so like charges align & repel → shatters). Molten or aqueous ionic salts have mobile ions and conduct electricity.
